These small MKVs are generated by `python3 generate_mkv.py` using GStreamer's
`matroskamux`, H.264 encoder, and the capture worker's timecode scale and
Comment tag schema. Session A includes B-frames and starts at timestamp 5 s;
session B restarts at timestamp 1 s with a different coded width. Both use
nanosecond UTC clock anchors.

`capture-irregular.mkv` has eight distinct H.264 frames with presentation
offsets of 0, 100, 100, 250, 300, 500, 500, and 700 ms. It exercises equal
timestamps and variable intervals. Its encoded timestamps are adjusted after
the encoder because x264 drops raw input frames with duplicate timestamps.

`capture-av-part1.mkv` and `capture-av-part2.mkv` are contiguous 1.8 s H.264 +
Opus fragments sharing a `captureSessionId` and `clockAnchor` (5.0–6.8 s and
6.8–8.6 s), generated by remuxing `capture-session-a.mkv` with PCMU-to-Opus
audio. `capture-legacy-pcmu.mkv` pairs the same video with raw PCMU (`A_MS/ACM`)
audio to verify video-only fallback for legacy recordings.

They test the browser demuxer, decoder, and MP4 clip export contracts, including
GStreamer's escaped `COMMENTS` representation. They are **not** recordings
emitted by the full capture worker and do not validate its RTSP or
`splitmuxsink` path. A full-worker fixture is still needed for that end-to-end
check.
